Get your instant quote

Our quote engine doesn't guess — it prices your phone by the value of its recoverable parts. A dead phone with a good screen is worth more than a working phone with a cracked one, because the screen is the single most valuable component.

  • Enter brand, model, age, and condition — takes under a minute
  • Honest condition = accurate quote; we re-check on arrival and only adjust if the description was off
  • Even a fully dead, cracked phone gets a minimum $5 — its circuit board alone is worth that in recoverable metals
2
Free, always

Ship it free or drop it off

Two ways to get your phone to us, both at zero cost:

  • Mail-in: print a prepaid shipping label from your quote confirmation, use any box, drop it at the post office
  • Drop boxes: secure collection boxes at partner schools, malls, and repair shops — scan the QR on the box to link the phone to your quote

Running a school fundraiser? Our collection drive program turns class competitions into e-waste hauls — we provide the boxes, posters, and a per-phone payout to the school.

3
Before anything else

Certified data erasure

The first thing that happens to every device — before disassembly, before grading — is a certified data wipe. Storage chips from phones that can't be wiped electronically are physically destroyed.

  • You receive an erasure confirmation by email for every device
  • No phone is opened, sold, or processed until its data is gone
  • Tip: sign out of your accounts and factory-reset before sending if you can — but if the phone is dead, don't worry, that's our job
4
The heart of ReVolt

Disassembly, testing & grading

Trained technicians take each phone apart completely. Every component is tested and given a grade — the same way the EV industry grades second-life car batteries:

  • Battery cells: capacity-tested and graded A/B/C — grade A/B cells go to small-electronics manufacturers, grade C to material recovery
  • Screens, cameras, ports, speakers: function-tested, graded, and listed for repair shops
  • Circuit boards: batched for certified precious-metal recovery
  • Frames & glass: sorted by material for recycling

Grading is what makes buyers trust used parts — every part we sell carries its test results.

5
Within 5 days

Payment & journey tracking

Once your phone arrives and matches its description, you're paid within 5 days — cash via e-transfer, or store credit worth 10% extra.

Then the part you'll want to screenshot: your journey tracker. Watch your phone's components find their new lives — "your battery now powers an LED work light", "your screen repaired another phone in Calgary". No other buyback tells you what actually happened to your device.

Questions we always get

FAQ

What if my phone is truly worthless?
No phone is. Even a waterlogged, shattered phone contains a circuit board with gold, silver, and copper — so our minimum payout is $5. If we can't reuse a single part, the materials still get recovered, and it still stays out of landfill.
Is my data really safe?
Yes — certified erasure happens before any other processing, and you get written confirmation per device. Chips that can't be electronically wiped are physically shredded. We'd rather destroy a sellable part than risk your data.
My battery is swollen — can I still send it?
Yes, but tell us in the comments box when you get your quote. Swollen batteries need special packaging (we'll ship you a fireproof bag) and go straight to certified recycling rather than reuse.
Do you take tablets, smartwatches, or laptops?
Phones are our focus today, but tablets and smartwatches use the same parts ecosystem — they're next on our roadmap. Join a school collection drive and we'll take them as part of the haul.
Why do you pay more than trade-in programs?
Because we monetize every component — battery, screen, camera, board, frame — while trade-ins only value phones they can resell whole. And we buy dead phones they flat-out reject. More revenue per phone means more payout per phone.

Our rule

The reuse waterfall

Every component takes the highest step available. Reuse always beats recycling — recycling still costs energy; reuse costs almost none.

🔋
Path 1 · Reuse

Battery cells → small-electronics manufacturers

A phone battery "dies" for phone duty at 70–80% of its original capacity — but that's plenty for less demanding devices. After capacity testing and grading, our cells power LED work lights, power banks, solar garden lights, and portable speakers.

This is the same proven model the EV industry uses: "retired" car batteries run grid storage for another decade. We're bringing that logic to the billions of phone cells sitting idle in drawers.

🔧
Path 2 · Reuse

Working parts → the repair market

Screens, cameras, speakers, vibration motors, charging ports, buttons, SIM trays — repair shops need all of it, constantly. A tested, graded screen from a dead phone is the single most valuable thing we sell, often worth more than the rest of the phone combined.

Every part we supply means one less new part manufactured — and one more phone somewhere that gets repaired instead of replaced. Our dead phones keep other people's phones alive.

⛏️
Path 3 · Recovery

Circuit boards → urban mining

Phone circuit boards are astonishingly rich: a tonne of boards contains more gold than a tonne of gold ore — plus silver, copper, and palladium. We batch boards and sell them to certified precious-metal recovery partners.

Every gram recovered is a gram that doesn't need to be mined, with all the habitat destruction, water use, and emissions mining brings.

♻️
Path 4 · Recycling

Frames, glass & everything else

Aluminum frames are recycled at ~95% energy savings versus producing new aluminum. Hardened phone glass can't become new phone glass, but it's downcycled into tiles, countertops, and abrasives. Rare-earth magnets from speakers and motors are recovered — rare earths are among the most supply-constrained materials on Earth.

The residue after all four paths? A small fraction of the phone's mass — and shrinking as recovery tech improves.

Why this works

The environmental math

The key idea: most of a phone's footprint happens before it's ever switched on.

🏭

Manufacturing dominates

Around 80% of a smartphone's lifetime emissions come from making it — mining, refining, fabrication — not from using it. Reusing parts attacks that 80%.

🔁

Reuse > recycling

Recycling melts things down and rebuilds them — that costs energy. Reuse skips the whole cycle: a reused screen has almost zero added footprint.

🗄️

Drawers are the blocker

Billions of phones sit unused worldwide. The materials exist — they're just parked. Paying cash is what finally opens the drawer.

⚖️

Displacement is the win

Every reused part displaces a newly manufactured one. That's the metric we optimize: not tonnes collected, but manufacturing avoided.

🌍 Nothing leaves our facility as garbage — only as a product, a part, or a raw material.

Why we exist

Mission & vision

🎯

Our mission

To get every idle phone out of the drawer and every one of its components into active use — paying people fairly for what they own, and proving that a dead phone is a resource, not waste.

🔭

Our vision

A world where "throwing away electronics" sounds as outdated as burning garbage in the backyard — where every device's end of life is simply the start of its components' next one. Organ donation, for phones.

How this started

Our story

💡

ReVolt was born at Shad 2026 at the University of Alberta, where our team was challenged to reimagine the lifecycle of everyday goods. We started with a question everyone in the room could answer: "how many old phones are in a drawer at your house right now?" Nobody said zero.

Then we learned two facts that wouldn't leave us alone: a "dead" phone battery still holds up to 80% of its capacity, and a tonne of phone circuit boards contains more gold than a tonne of gold ore. The waste wasn't being thrown away — it was being stored, invisibly, in millions of homes. Nobody was paying people to open the drawer.

So that became the business: pay cash for the phones nobody else wants, take them apart with care, and route every component to whoever values it most. You've read the rest of the site — that's the whole idea. It just needed someone to do it.

Our principles

What we believe

♻️ Reuse before recycling

Recycling is the fallback, never the goal. A part in use is worth ten parts melted down — so every component takes the highest step on our waterfall it can.

💸 Pay people fairly

The drawer stays shut because nobody rewards opening it. Fair cash payouts aren't a marketing cost — they're the engine of the whole model.

🔍 Prove it, don't claim it

"Eco-friendly" is cheap to say. We show every seller exactly where their phone's parts went. If we can't prove a green claim, we don't make it.

🔒 Privacy is non-negotiable

Certified data erasure before any processing, always. We'd rather destroy a sellable part than risk a customer's data.
