Where your phone goes

Every phone is fully disassembled, and every component is sold to whoever values it most. Our goal is for nothing to idle and nothing to be landfilled.

Path 1

Battery's second life

A "dead" phone battery still holds up to 70 to 80% of its capacity, plenty for LED lights, power banks, and small electronics. We test, grade, and sell cells to manufacturers.

Path 2

Parts to repair shops

Screens, cameras, speakers, and charging ports keep other phones alive. Your dead phone becomes someone else's repair, with no new parts manufactured.

Path 3

Urban mining

Circuit boards go to certified recovery for gold, silver, copper, and palladium. A tonne of boards holds more gold than a tonne of ore, and none of it needs digging up.

Path 4

Frame & glass recycling

Aluminum frames are recycled at roughly 95% energy savings versus new. Hardened glass gets downcycled into tiles and surfaces. The last gram is accounted for.

Path 5

Dead component disposal

Battery cells that fail testing, swollen cells, and anything else with zero recovery value are meant for certified hazardous-waste recyclers, funded by the manufacturer, not landfilled by us directly. That's the design, not a measured result yet.

Two ways to get your phone to us, both at zero cost:

  • Mail-in: print a prepaid shipping label from your quote confirmation, use any box, drop it at the post office
  • Drop boxes: secure collection boxes at partner schools, malls, and repair shops. Scan the QR code on the box to link the phone to your quote

Running a school fundraiser? Our collection drive program turns class competitions into e-waste hauls. We provide the boxes, the posters, and a per-phone payout to the school.

3
Before anything else

Certified data erasure

The first thing that happens to every device, before disassembly and before grading, is a certified data wipe. Storage chips from phones that can't be wiped electronically get physically destroyed.

  • You receive an erasure confirmation by email for every device
  • No phone is opened, sold, or processed until its data is gone
  • Tip: sign out of your accounts and factory reset before sending if you can. If the phone is dead, don't worry, that's our job
4
The heart of GreenVolt

Disassembly, testing & grading

Trained technicians take each phone apart completely. Every component is tested and given a grade, the same way the EV industry grades second-life car batteries:

  • Battery cells: capacity-tested and graded A, B, or C. Grade A and B cells go to small-electronics manufacturers, grade C goes to material recovery
  • Screens, cameras, ports, speakers: function-tested, graded, and listed for repair shops
  • Circuit boards: batched for certified precious-metal recovery
  • Frames & glass: sorted by material for recycling

Grading is what makes buyers trust used parts. Every part we sell carries its test results.

Within 5 days

Payment & journey tracking

Once your phone arrives and matches its description, you're paid within 5 days: cash by e-transfer, or store credit worth 10% extra.

Then the part you'll want to screenshot is your journey tracker. It shows you where your components ended up, like your battery now powering an LED work light, or your screen repairing another phone in Calgary. No other buyback tells you what actually happened to your device.

Questions we always get

FAQ

What if my phone is truly worthless?
No phone is. Even a waterlogged, shattered phone contains a circuit board with gold, silver, and copper, so our minimum payout is $5. If we can't reuse a single part, the materials are still meant to be recovered and kept out of landfill, that's the goal we're built around.
Is my data really safe?
Yes. Certified erasure happens before any other processing, and you get written confirmation per device. Chips that can't be electronically wiped are physically shredded. We'd rather destroy a sellable part than risk your data.
My battery is swollen, can I still send it?
Yes, but tell us in the comments box when you get your quote. Swollen batteries need special packaging (we'll ship you a fireproof bag) and go straight to certified recycling rather than reuse.
Do you take tablets, smartwatches, or laptops?
Phones are our focus today, but tablets and smartwatches use the same parts ecosystem, and they're next on our roadmap. Join a school collection drive and we'll take them as part of the haul.
Why do you pay more than trade-in programs?
Because we monetize every component (battery, screen, camera, board, frame) while trade-ins only value phones they can resell whole. We also buy dead phones they flat-out reject. More revenue per phone means more payout per phone.

Our rule

The reuse waterfall

Every component takes the highest step available. Reuse always beats recycling, since recycling still costs energy while reuse costs almost none.

Path 1 · Reuse

Battery cells → small-electronics manufacturers

A phone battery "dies" for phone duty at 70 to 80% of its original capacity, but that's plenty for less demanding devices. After capacity testing and grading, our cells power LED work lights, power banks, solar garden lights, and portable speakers.

This is the same proven model the EV industry uses: "retired" car batteries run grid storage for another decade. We're bringing that logic to the billions of phone cells sitting idle in drawers.

Path 2 · Reuse

Working parts → the repair market

Screens, cameras, speakers, vibration motors, charging ports, buttons, and SIM trays: repair shops need all of it, constantly. A tested, graded screen from a dead phone is the single most valuable thing we sell, often worth more than the rest of the phone combined.

Every part we supply means one less new part manufactured, and one more phone somewhere that gets repaired instead of replaced. Our dead phones keep other people's phones alive.

Path 3 · Recovery

Circuit boards → urban mining

Phone circuit boards are astonishingly rich: a tonne of boards contains more gold than a tonne of gold ore, plus silver, copper, and palladium. We batch boards and sell them to certified precious-metal recovery partners.

Every gram recovered is a gram that doesn't need to be mined, with all the habitat destruction, water use, and emissions mining brings.

Path 4 · Recycling

Frames, glass & everything else

Aluminum frames are recycled at ~95% energy savings versus producing new aluminum. Hardened phone glass can't become new phone glass, but it's downcycled into tiles, countertops, and abrasives. Rare-earth magnets from speakers and motors are recovered, since rare earths are among the most supply-constrained materials on Earth.

The residue after all five paths is a small fraction of the phone's mass, and it's shrinking as recovery tech improves.

Path 5 · Disposal

Dead components & certified hazardous-waste recovery

Not everything clears testing. Battery cells that fail capacity checks, swollen or physically damaged cells, and any material left with zero resale or recovery value still can't just go in the trash.

These are meant for certified domestic recyclers, Call2Recycle for batteries, EPRA for other e-waste, funded by the manufacturer's producer Environmental Handling Fees, not by us or by you. It's the one path with no recovery value, and our goal is to keep it handled, not landfilled, though we haven't processed a device yet to prove that out.

Why this works

The environmental math

The key idea: most of a phone's footprint happens before it's ever switched on.

Manufacturing dominates

Around 80% of a smartphone's lifetime emissions come from making it (mining, refining, fabrication), not from using it. Reusing parts attacks that 80%.

Reuse > recycling

Recycling melts things down and rebuilds them, and that costs energy. Reuse skips the whole cycle: a reused screen has almost zero added footprint.

Drawers are the blocker

Billions of phones sit unused worldwide. The materials exist, they're just parked, and paying cash is what finally opens the drawer.

Displacement is the win

Every reused part displaces a newly manufactured one. That's the metric we optimize: not tonnes collected, but manufacturing avoided.

Our goal: nothing leaves our facility as garbage, only as a product, a part, or a raw material.
The real alternative

GreenVolt versus sending it anywhere else

Same broken phone, two different endings. Here's what typically happens depending on where you send it.

With GreenVolt

Every component gets a destination

  1. Photographed and graded into one of three tiers: battery fault only, screen fault only, or beyond economical repair
  2. Disassembled by hand, not shredded
  3. Battery, screen, camera, and board each routed to whoever values that part most
  4. Anything truly unusable goes to certified domestic recyclers (Call2Recycle, EPRA), funded by the manufacturer, not you

Our goal is as close to 0% landfill as a physical process can get. That's a target we're built around, not a measured result, since we haven't processed a device yet.

A typical trade-in or generic recycler

Only resellable whole devices get attention

  1. Tested for resale as a complete, working device
  2. If it doesn't power on or can't be resold whole, it's rejected or bundled into generic e-waste streams
  3. Individual components are rarely harvested, since the business model is reselling whole phones
  4. Bundled e-waste often ends up outside formal recycling entirely; for small electronics specifically, an estimated 8% is discarded straight to landfill, and up to 20% more is exported, much of it processed informally

Globally, only 22.3% of e-waste is documented as formally collected and recycled (UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024). The remaining share gets traded, stockpiled, or processed informally, sometimes through unsafe methods like open burning to recover metals.

Why we exist

Mission & vision

Our mission

To get every idle phone out of the drawer and every one of its components into active use, paying people fairly for what they own, and proving that a dead phone is a resource, not waste.

Our vision

A world where "throwing away electronics" sounds as outdated as burning garbage in the backyard, where every device's end of life is simply the start of its components' next one. Organ donation, for phones.

How this started

Our story

GreenVolt was born at Shad 2026 at the University of Alberta, where our team was challenged to reimagine the lifecycle of everyday goods. We started with a question everyone in the room could answer: "how many old phones are in a drawer at your house right now?" Nobody said zero.

Then we learned two facts that wouldn't leave us alone: a "dead" phone battery still holds up to 80% of its capacity, and a tonne of phone circuit boards contains more gold than a tonne of gold ore. The waste wasn't being thrown away. It was being stored, invisibly, in millions of homes. Nobody was paying people to open the drawer.

So that became the business: pay cash for the phones nobody else wants, take them apart with care, and route every component to whoever values it most. You've read the rest of the site. That's the whole idea, it just needed someone to do it.

Our principles

What we believe

Reuse before recycling

Recycling is the fallback, never the goal. A part in use is worth ten parts melted down, so every component takes the highest step on our waterfall it can.

Pay people fairly

The drawer stays shut because nobody rewards opening it. Fair cash payouts aren't a marketing cost. They're the engine of the whole model.

Prove it, don't claim it

"Eco-friendly" is cheap to say. We show every seller exactly where their phone's parts went. If we can't prove a green claim, we don't make it.

Privacy is non-negotiable

Certified data erasure before any processing, always. We'd rather destroy a sellable part than risk a customer's data.
